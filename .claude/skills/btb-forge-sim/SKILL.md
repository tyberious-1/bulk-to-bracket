# Forge simulator (test-only, runs in Docker)

> **Summary:** Forge is an open-source MTG engine with AI players. We run it headless in a Docker container to play two or four built decks against each other and read the game logs. It is a dev tool only: never part of the app, nothing in `js/` depends on it. It answers "does this deck function" (is the commander cast, are there early plays, how often is the deck stuck), not "is this deck good".
> **STOP here unless** you are running a batch, changing `dev/tools/forge-sim/`, or reading its results.

## Where things are

- `dev/tools/forge-sim/Dockerfile`: Java 17 image, downloads a pinned Forge release (2.0.16, about 300 MB) inside the image. Build once: `docker build -t btb-forge dev/tools/forge-sim`. Docker Desktop must be running (the daemon is not started for you).
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/to_dck.js`: `node to_dck.js <deck json from a harness dump> <outdir>` writes a Forge Commander `.dck` (commander in `[Commander]`, deck `Name=` is the commander's name so the parser can match player names to decks).
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/run_sim.sh <games> <log> <deck.dck> <deck.dck> [more...]`: 2 decks = a duel, 4 = a pod. Prints the parser summary at the end.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/parse.js <log>`: per deck: commander cast count and turn, spells in the first 5 own turns, nothing cast by own turn 3, mulligans and kept hand size, land drops in the first 4 and 6 own turns, wins, games with no winner, wins by seat.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/howwon.js <log>`: how each player was eliminated, from the Damage and Life lines. The damage totals are NOT reliable (they appear to add up over several turns); use it for the kind of damage and which cards were hitting, not amounts.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/timeline.js <log> <deck name fragment> [turns]`: per game, what one deck did on each of its own turns (land played or NO LAND, spells cast with targets, who killed it and when). Use it to read losses instead of the raw log.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/pace.js <log> [<log>...]`: per deck and own turn 1 to 8, average spells cast, share of turns with no spell, games reaching the turn (own turns only). Result 2026-11-17 over 6 pods: every deck, ours and the precons, casts about 1 spell per own turn from turn 3 and first attacks on own turn 5 to 6; ours are not visibly slower than the precons by these measures. Spell count says nothing about spell strength.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/deaths.js <log> [deck fragment]`: per elimination, whose turn it was, the attackers that turn, the victim's life going into that turn, creatures the killer had cast. The block counts it prints are NOT reliable (one `didn't block` line can cover several attackers); ignore them. 2026-11-17 over Run A and B, 33 deaths of our four builds: 27 came when the victim's life going into the killing turn was 13 or less, so they were worn down over earlier turns first; the killing turns had 3 to 13 or more attackers (Quintorius: Spirit Tokens, Shelob: spiders, Sethron: Minotaur tokens).
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/gamesum.js <log>`: per game, winner, time, spells cast per deck, commander casts and times it left the battlefield, cards drawn by effects per deck and which cards drew them, attack counts per attacker>defender, lowest life, targeted spells. Set `FORGE_CLOCK_MS` if the run used `-c`.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/work/` is git-ignored: decks (`work/decks/*.dck`), logs, harness exports.
- To get a full deck with lands from the builder, use a copy of `dump_decks.js` whose output is `{ cards: deck.map(c => c.name) }` (keep lands), with `ONLY_COMMANDERS` and `MODE_OVERRIDE`; the 99 cards come out without the commander.
- Forge ships the commander precons: `/forge/res/quest/commanderprecons/*.dck` inside the image (copy one out with a docker run mount, then set its `Name=` to its commander).

- `dev/tools/forge-sim/plan_turns.js <log>` and `win_cards.js <log>` (2026-11-18): per deck and own turn, spells, attackers, commander triggers, tokens made; and which cards were cast in wins vs losses plus combat-hit sources. Both assume two or four decks named after their commanders. Result notes: `dev/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-18-forge-sethron-shelob-duels.md`, which also records an unresolved question about Shelob's Food copies attacking.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/board.js <log>... [--deck <frag[,frag]>] [--per-game] [--pool <label>]` (2026-11-18): the creature board still standing when a deck DIED, and how much of its power never attacked (damage available, not just damage dealt). The log has NO battlefield-entry event, so the board is rebuilt as cast creatures minus seen exits: **a lower bound that ignores tokens, static buffs and tapped state** (go-wide decks are undercounted most). Needs card data beyond the collection: run `python3 dev/tools/forge-sim/fetch_deck_cards.py dev/tools/forge-sim/work/precon_cards.json <deck.dck>...` from `work/decks/` first (strips Forge's `Name|SET|n` suffixes). Always check its line "cast cards with NO card data" is about 0: an earlier draft skipped unknown cards silently, so every precon read as an empty board (26 of 51 losses) and the true figure was 7 of 51.
- `dev/tools/forge-sim/common.js`: shared helper `isCommanderCast(player, card)` used by `parse.js` and `gamesum.js` (fixed 2026-11-18; `gamesum.js` used to hard-code four commander names, so it printed `{}` for any other pod, and `parse.js` could not match a suffixed player name). A deck's `Name=` must be its commander's full name; a mirror pod (the same list four times) adds a one-character suffix (" A", " #2") so the seats differ. In a mirror, `gamesum.js` cannot tell whose copy left the battlefield once two players have cast it (the log has no owner) and reports it under "<name> (shared name)".

## Which decks are builder output and which are Forge precons (mixed up once on 2026-11-17, do not repeat)

- **Builder output (our decks):** Sethron, Shelob (and the `E` versions), High Perfect Morcant, Greasefang. Only these say anything about the builder.
- **Forge precons (from `/forge/res/quest/commanderprecons`, benchmarks only):** Living Energy (Saheeli, Radiant Creator, DRC), Lorehold Spirit (Quintorius, History Chaser, SOC), Animated Army (Bello, Bard of the Brambles, BLC), Blame Game (Nelly Borca, Impulsive Accuser, MKC; a goad/politics deck, a poor fit for the sim). Copy a precon out with a docker run mount and set its `Name=` to its commander.
- A finding about a precon's plan (Saheeli's energy engine, Quintorius's graveyard draw) is a finding about that precon, not about the builder.
- Noise floor, 2026-11-18: a mirror of four identical Quintorius lists (4 games, `-mc on -c 900`, `work/podLoreMirror4.txt`) went 0, 0, 2, 2 wins; early spells in the first 5 own turns ranged 3.5 to 6.3 and land drops in the first 6 turns 4.0 to 6.0. A 2 to 3 spell or 1.5 land-drop gap between two decks in a batch this size is inside the noise.

## Forge `sim` options (from its own help, 2.0.16)

`-d <decks>` decks by name or file; `-D <dir>` deck folder; `-n <N>` games (ignores `-m`); `-m <M>` match of M games; `-t <Bracket|RoundRobin|Swiss>` tournament over all decks; `-p <P>` players per match (tournaments); `-f <format>` we use `commander`; `-s <seed>` RNG seed (same shuffle, so builder versions can be compared on identical games); `-a <profiles>` AI profile per player; `-c <seconds>` clock before a draw (default 120); `-mc <on|max|off>` Flat Monte Carlo AI (off by default); `-q` quiet (result only; our parser needs the full log, so do not use it).

## What Forge's own docs say (read from /forge/docs in the image, 2026-11-17)

- `AI.md`: the AI is NOT trained; it uses heuristics. It is "best with Aggro and midrange decks", "poor to OK in control decks" and "pretty bad for most combo decks". Hardcoded logic exists for some single cards. Consequence for us: a win rate says how well the AI pilots that kind of deck, so a go-wide aggro precon will look better than a value or combo deck. Judge "does the deck function", not "is it good".
- `AI.md` on command-line runs: meant for scripted tests of decks and 100s of games; "games can lag and become almost unbearably long when the AI has a lot to think about"; set it up and walk away; results are printed at the end. `-d` decks are searched in the `-f` format's folder (`<userdata>/decks/commander/` for Commander); `-c` is the clock before a draw (default 120 seconds); `-m` is best-of matches and overrides `-n`; `-q` prints only results.
- The docs say NOTHING about how the starting player or seats are chosen, and nothing about the seed beyond the help text. `Frequently-Asked-Questions.md` is GUI troubleshooting, bug reporting (check the issue tracker; read the card and rulings first because Forge often enforces the rules correctly) and development; it has nothing on simulation. `Missing-Cards-in-Forge.md` (list of unimplemented cards) was NOT read, and the check of our decks against Forge's card scripts was NOT completed (the card scripts are in `res/cardsfolder/cardsfolder.zip`, not loose files). So whether every card in our decks is implemented is unverified.
- Logs contain `TimeoutException` stack traces from `AiController.chooseSpellAbilitiesToPlayFromList` (the AI ran out of thinking time on a decision, then logged "AI Attack declaration invalid, defaulting to..."): 5 across the 36 pod games on 2026-11-17, so rare, effect unknown.

## AI strength: we were running the weakest setting (found 2026-11-17)

- `-mc <on|max|off>` turns on Forge's Flat Monte Carlo layer. It is **off by default in simulations**, so every batch before 2026-11-17 used pure heuristics. `-a <profile ...>` sets an AI profile per player: Cautious, Default, Experimental, Hard, Hardest, Reckless (in `/forge/res/ai/*.ai`). Hardest raises the MC budgets (decision 1500 ms vs 450, 6 rollouts vs 4, turn budget 6000 ms vs 2000). The profiles' `FLAT_MC_*` values only bite when the MC layer is on. There is no trained/neural model; MC is a search refinement over the same heuristics.
- Measured on 4 duel games (Morcant vs Greasefang), `-mc on` against the default: wall clock 75 s vs 19 s (about 4x slower); draws logged 18 vs 2; discards 18 vs 5; targeted spells 13 vs 8; turn lines 107 vs 87. **The stronger AI uses optional abilities and removal far more**, so a card that looks dead under the default AI may not be dead.
- Run with options through `FORGE_OPTS` (e.g. `FORGE_OPTS="-mc on -c 900" bash dev/tools/forge-sim/run_sim.sh ...`); `run_sim.sh` then sets `FORGE_CLOCK_MS` so the parsers (`parse.js`, `howwon.js`, `timeline.js`) call a game a timeout only at YOUR clock, not at 120 s. Set `FORGE_CLOCK_MS` yourself when you run a parser on an old log.
- Result 2026-11-17, 4-deck pod (Morcant, Greasefang, SethronE, ShelobE) with `-mc on -c 900`, 4 games (`work/podMC4.txt`) against the default-AI Run B, 8 games (`work/podB.txt`): game length 32 to 211 s against 13 to 64 s; turn lines per game about 59 against about 58; targeted spells per game about 4.5 against about 5 (no rise in removal); extra cards drawn per game: Greasefang 3.25 vs 1.75, Morcant 2.25 vs 0.5, Sethron 1.75 vs 1.25, Shelob 1.0 vs 1.1. Morcant's draws came only from Night's Whisper, Harmonize and Infectious Inquiry (the conditional draw cards drew nothing; whether they triggered is unchecked). 4 games is noise-sized: not a deck-quality verdict. 8-game Morcant vs Greasefang duel with MC on: `work/duelMC8.txt` (Morcant 7, Greasefang 1).
- Combat is in the log: `Combat:` lines (attack, block, `didn't block`), `Damage:` lines, `Zone Change` for deaths (no cause). Of 173 combat damage lines in the MC pod, 159 hit players and 14 hit creatures. Matching a death to its cause is not built.
- Consequence: any finding that a card "did nothing" must be re-checked with `-mc on` before it is treated as a deck property. With MC on, raise `-c` well above the 120 s default or four-player games will nearly all end as timeout draws.

## What the log contains, and does not

Contains: turn and phase lines (`Turn: Turn N (player)`), `Land: X played Y`, `Add To Stack: X cast Y` (also triggers and activations), `Mana:` taps, `Resolve Stack`, `Damage:` (source card, combat or non-combat, target), `Life:` changes, `Zone Change` (graveyard moves), `Mulligan:` (`has mulliganed down to N cards`, `has kept a hand of N cards`), and `Game Outcome:` (winner; losers all read "life total reached 0").
Does NOT contain: the cards in a hand, draws, or a commander-damage loss reason. Opening hands would need a Forge source change; to study hands, deal them yourself in Node from the deck list.

## Rules for reading results

- A missed land drop is an own turn with no land played. Missed drops happen in real games; only a pattern across many games means anything.
- Win rate is a sanity check only. With 4 players each deck is expected to win 25% (5 of 20, give or take about 2); any single batch of 20 is noise-sized. Look at whether the deck functions.
- Seat number is the order the decks were listed. Observed (3 pod batches, 36 games): the first-listed deck took turn 1 in 28 of 36 games (random would be about 9); reversing the order moved turn 1 to the other decks, so it follows list position, not the deck. Seats 3 and 4 almost never started. Why Forge does this is NOT established (not in its docs; source not read). Wins did not follow seat: Quintorius won from seat 4 and from seat 1; Sethron lost from seat 1 and from seat 4. In a 20-game duel the second-listed deck took turn 1 in 15 games. Do not state a starting-player rule as fact without checking Forge's source.
- **Seat order does not decide wins (the user, 2026-11-20; stop raising it).** Seat is only list position, and who takes turn 1 is not a win predictor. Evidence: 834 duel games (`seat-order-does-not-matter-in-duels` memory), and an 80-game 4-deck pod (`work/pod4_even_A..D.txt`, seat order rotated across four 20-game batches): total wins by seat 18, 20, 24, 18 (flat), while one deck (Ghalta) won 42 of 80 from every seat. A deck's win count follows the deck, so rotate the order across batches if you like, but do not report seat as a cause or caveat.
- Compare builder versions with the same seed and the same decks; compare against precons as a reference.
- A game that runs the full 120 s clock (`-c`, `Game Result ... ended in 12000x ms`) is a DRAW: every player's `Game Outcome` line reads "has won because all opponents have lost", yet `Game Result` still names one player. parse.js and howwon.js now count these as timeouts, not wins (found 2026-11-17). Corrected: the 20-game pod was Sethron 1, Shelob 1, Saheeli 1, Quintorius 12, with 5 timeouts; Morcant's and Saheeli's Run A "wins" in games 4 and 6 were timeouts. How Forge picks the named player is NOT established.
- Elimination: a player is out when a `Life:` line takes them to 0 or below, during another player's attack. The last death ends the game on the winner's turn. The `Turn N` counter is global (each seat every 4th line while all four live); the `Game Outcome: Turn N` value does not match it (game 1: 41 Turn lines, outcome says 21), so do not use it.
- Card IDs in parentheses tell same-named cards apart (two Arcane Signets in one game); `Mana:` lines name no player.
- Targeted spells: `cast X targeting [Y]` then a `Zone Change` of Y shows removal that landed; counterspells and similar show no zone change. The log shows what the AI did, not why.
- Do not state guesses as facts in reports: say what the log shows, then label inferences.

## Operating notes (learned the hard way)

- Run long batches with `run_in_background`. A 4-player game takes about 1 to 2 minutes (20 games took about 40 minutes); a duel about 5 seconds. The laptop sleeping (lid closed) pauses Docker and the run; do not close the lid mid-run.
- `run_sim.sh` has a stall watchdog (added 2026-11-18): if the log stops growing for `FORGE_STALL` seconds (default 600; `FORGE_POLL`, default 30, is the poll interval) it stops its own container and still parses what was written; `parse.js` drops an unfinished last game. Reason: on 2026-11-17 game 7 of an 8-game pod stalled before turn 1 (no turn line, Java CPU almost flat for an hour) and the `timeout 3000` inside the container never fired, because that clock does not advance while the laptop sleeps. The cause of the stall itself is not known. The watchdog counts awake time only.
- Check `docker ps` before starting a run. An interrupted command can leave a container running that keeps writing to the same log file and corrupts it. Stop your own containers with `docker stop <name>` and `pkill -f run_sim.sh`.
- Do not leave a bare `cat` or an unterminated heredoc in a shell call; it hangs.
- Build with the Docker daemon running only; Forge needs no network at run time.
- Keep batches small: about 8 games for a four-player pod (about 15 minutes), 20 for a duel (about 2 minutes). 20-game pods took about 40 minutes.
- The winner of a game is the `Game Result: Game N ended ... X has won!` line (exactly one per game). `Game Outcome: ... has won because all opponents have lost` can print several times in one game, so counting it gives wrong winners (found 2026-11-17; parse.js and howwon.js now read Game Result).
- Data from the first runs (not a benchmark; 20-game pod of Sethron, Shelob, Saheeli from Living Energy, Quintorius from Lorehold Spirit, always listed in that order): wins 1, 5, 1, 13; each seat number was always the same deck; Sethron (listed first) took turn 1 in 18 of 20 games; every deck cast its commander in 19 or 20 games. The earlier duel (Sethron listed first, Shelob second): Sethron 16 wins, Shelob took turn 1 in 15 of 20 games. Whether seat, turn order or deck drives the wins is not established.
